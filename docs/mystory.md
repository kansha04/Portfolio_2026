# About Me

My name is Tinovonga Mushayi. I was born in Zimbabwe in 2008, which makes me 18, and moved to South Africa in late 2012. When I was 14, my uncle who was a self-taught programmer told me about web development, and that was when I started getting into coding and tech. I followed Udemy courses and YouTube tutorials on HTML and CSS, building simple pages that I was so proud of at the time (it's always funny to look back at them).

During that year, I learned that my family and I would be moving to Belgium. It was going to be a big change, and I was very excited for it. I packed up my desktop in a suitcase, hoped it would survive the flight, and after a long journey, we finally arrived in Leuven, where we still live today. At the time, being in a completely new country felt both daunting and exhilarating. Learning Dutch and adjusting to the new school system was my first priority at the start, which caused me to put coding on the back burner for a solid few months.

Wanting to go deeper into my coding journey, I started taking courses on platforms like Codecademy and freeCodeCamp. This is where I began learning languages like JavaScript and Python. I eventually finished some of those courses, but I felt like I had hit a plateau, as though I wasn't really understanding what I was learning. It stayed like this until around the summer of 2025, when I realized that focusing so much on syntax was a waste of time, and that building things that challenged me and that I enjoyed was much more fulfilling.

I continued designing websites and building projects in other languages, such as a stock ticker checker in Python, a Santa Helper in C#, a full-stack webshop concept (EuroSpec), and a website for my dad's business. I gradually realized how much I enjoyed seeing how technology works and how it can be applied to other fields, which ultimately led me to choose Business Supporting Computer Science as my high school track.

This past summer was my biggest leap, as I had the chance to work on a new project that some family members were collaborating on. This was my first exposure to working on a real, production-level project. It was a huge step up from what I was used to, but I dove straight in and got to work. I started self-assigning tasks and learning how actual production systems work and how they are built. Learning from people who have a lot more experience in their fields was truly eye-opening, and I always try to learn something from them.

I am excited for my next chapter, where I will start working as a Student Software Engineer at Cambiar.ai, contributing to projects with real users and tackling tougher challenges, as well as continuing with the personal projects I have planned.
