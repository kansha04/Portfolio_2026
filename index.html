● In pilot · not yet public Team projectHealthMessaging

ScriptHelper

A Telegram and WhatsApp assistant that answers Zimbabwean pharmacists’ medicine questions, and refuses when it can’t answer safely.

Pharmacists need quick, reliable information about the medicines in front of them. The tools that could help either don’t live on the channels they actually use, or they confidently make things up. ScriptHelper, a project I worked on with a small team, is built to solve both problems.

Every clinical reply is grounded in a source it can cite: the FDA’s product labelling, fetched live, and, where a deployment switches it on, Zimbabwe’s EDLIZ 2020 essential-medicines list. If no source covers the question, it declines rather than guesses. The pilot currently answers from the FDA labelling alone. It’s decision-support, not clinical advice.

My story
(02)

My story, in my own words.

Where it started

My name is Tinovonga Mushayi. I was born in Zimbabwe in 2008, which makes me 18, and moved to South Africa in late 2012. When I was 14, a relative who was a self-taught programmer told me about web development, and that was when I started getting into coding and tech. I followed Udemy courses and YouTube tutorials on HTML and CSS, building simple pages that I was so proud of at the time (it’s always funny to look back at them).

Leuven

Not long after, I learned that my family and I would be moving to Belgium. It was going to be a big change, and I was very excited for it. I packed up my desktop in a suitcase, hoped it would survive the flight, and after a long journey, we finally arrived in Leuven, where we still live today. At the time, being in a completely new country felt both daunting and exhilarating. Learning Dutch and adjusting to the new school system was my first priority at the start, which caused me to put coding on the back burner for a solid few months.

The plateau

Wanting to go deeper into my coding journey, I started taking courses on platforms like Codecademy and freeCodeCamp. This is where I began learning languages like JavaScript and Python. I eventually finished some of those courses, but I felt like I had hit a plateau, as though I wasn’t really understanding what I was learning. It stayed like this until around the summer of 2025, when I realized that focusing so much on syntax was a waste of time, and that building things that challenged me and that I enjoyed was much more fulfilling.

Building

I continued designing websites and started building more ambitious projects, such as a car dealership site (EuroSpec), a live earthquake map built on the USGS data feed (Seismic Core), and the website for my dad’s business. I gradually realized how much I enjoyed seeing how technology works and how it can be applied to other fields, which ultimately led me to choose Business Supporting Computer Science as my high school track.

This summer (2026)

This past summer was my biggest leap: I was a main contributor to building ScriptHelper joining a 3-person engineering team which took it from a initial prototype into a live pilot. It was my first exposure to a real, production-level system, and a huge step up from what I was used to, but I dove straight in. I started self-assigning tasks and learning how production systems are built, and I learned a great deal from people with far more experience than me.

NOW

I’m working part time as a Student Software Engineer at Cambiar.ai, contributing to projects with real users and tackling tougher challenges, as well as starting to work on harder projects I have planned within the aviation.

One thing I don’t bend on
→

Urgency.

Moving fast and being careful aren’t opposites to me: I ship quickly because the safety checks are already built in. When something matters, waiting is a cost too, so I get a careful first version out and improve it in the open.
